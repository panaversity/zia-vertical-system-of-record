# Abstention

**"That is not in this corpus" is a correct answer.** It is never an error, never a failure, and
never a reason to fall back on what you happen to know.

This corpus is finite on purpose. Its value is that everything inside it was checked by someone
who is accountable for it. The moment an answer is stitched together from general knowledge, the
reader cannot tell which sentences were checked and which were guessed — and the whole corpus
drops to the confidence level of the guess.

## When answering from the corpus

- Answer only from what the documents say. If they are silent, say they are silent.
- **Scope-adjacent questions are the dangerous ones.** A question about something far outside the
  corpus is easy to decline. A question one inch outside — the neighbouring rule, the next
  jurisdiction, last year's version — is the one that gets answered from memory. Slow down there.
- Partial coverage is partial: answer the covered part, name the uncovered part, stop.
- When declining, be useful: name what the corpus *does* cover nearby, and what document would
  have to exist for the answer to be available.

## When writing into the corpus

The same rule governs authoring, and it is easier to break:

- Do not complete a half-covered topic with what you already know. A source that covers four of
  five conditions produces a document about four conditions, plus a note to the owner about the
  fifth.
- Do not add the obvious background paragraph. If it were in a source, it would have a source.
- Do not smooth over a gap in a source with a transition sentence that quietly asserts something.

If the gap matters, the fix is to go and get the source — not to write around it.
